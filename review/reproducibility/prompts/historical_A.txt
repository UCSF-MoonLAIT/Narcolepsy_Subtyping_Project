=== ICSD-3 DIAGNOSTIC CRITERIA — CLINICAL REFERENCE ONLY ===

The information below is provided ONLY to help interpret diagnoses, symptoms, and historical testing documented in the clinical record.
Do NOT use these criteria to independently diagnose NT1, NT2, or IH. Do NOT construct a new diagnosis by combining individual symptoms or test findings when the longitudinal record does not document that diagnosis.
The study task is to abstract the diagnosis supported and documented in the longitudinal clinical record. Historical diagnostic workup may be incomplete, unavailable, or documented only indirectly.

[ Narcolepsy Type 1 (NT1) — both A and B required ]
A. Daily irrepressible daytime sleepiness or unintended daytime sleep episodes for ≥3 months.
B. At least ONE of:
   (1) Cataplexy AND MSLT mean sleep latency ≤8 min AND ≥2 sleep-onset REM periods (SOREMPs). A SOREMP on the preceding nocturnal PSG (REM within 15 min of sleep onset) may replace one of the daytime SOREMPs.
   (2) CSF hypocretin-1 (orexin) ≤110 pg/mL by radioimmunoassay, or <1/3 of mean normal.

[ Narcolepsy Type 2 (NT2) — ALL 5 required ]
1. Chronic daily EDS for ≥3 months.
2. MSLT MSL ≤8 min AND ≥2 SOREMPs (PSG SOREMP may replace one daytime SOREMP).
3. No cataplexy.
4. CSF hypocretin >110 pg/mL OR not measured.
5. Symptoms and MSLT not better explained by insufficient sleep, OSA, DSPS, or medication/substance use/withdrawal.

[ Cataplexy ]
Typical = brief (s to <2 min), bilateral loss of muscle tone triggered by strong emotion (especially laughter), with preserved consciousness.
Atypical = partial, prolonged, or non-emotion-triggered weakness (does NOT fulfill NT1 B(1)).
Explicit cataplexy diagnosis by a sleep specialist should be interpreted in the context of the overall longitudinal record, even if detailed symptom descriptions are unavailable.

[ Idiopathic Hypersomnia (IH) — clinical reference only ]

Idiopathic hypersomnia is characterized in ICSD-3 by chronic excessive daytime sleepiness for ≥3 months, absence of cataplexy, fewer than 2 SOREMPs, and either MSLT mean sleep latency ≤8 minutes or total 24-hour sleep time ≥660 minutes. 
Insufficient sleep and other explanations for hypersomnolence should be excluded. These features are provided ONLY to help interpret clinical documentation, historical testing, and specialist assessments in the longitudinal record. 
Do NOT independently diagnose IH by assembling these features or by determining that the patient satisfies the ICSD-3 criteria.
Assign IH when the longitudinal clinical record documents IH as the established diagnosis. Historical diagnostic testing may be incomplete or unavailable in the provided notes.

=== HIERARCHY OF EVIDENCE ===

When evidence conflicts, use the following priority:

Highest:
- explicit specialist diagnostic assessment
- documented outside specialist diagnosis
- documented objective testing interpreted within the clinical context
- explicit diagnostic revision or clarification

Intermediate:
- repeated longitudinal specialist management
- repeated independent clinical assessments documenting an established diagnosis

Lower:
- isolated historical mentions
- ICD codes
- problem lists
- medication use alone

=== EVIDENCE TRIAD ===

The evidence triad is a framework for organizing the available evidence. It is NOT a checklist. Evidence should be weighed collectively in the context of the complete longitudinal record.

(E1) CLINICAL DESCRIPTION in narrative (HPI / Interim History / Assessment): EDS, prolonged sleep duration, sleep inertia, unintended sleep episodes, cataplexy features, sleep paralysis, hypnagogic/hypnopompic hallucinations, or idiopathic hypersomnia as an active documented diagnosis. 
(E2) SPECIALIST MANAGEMENT: specialist evaluation or longitudinal management documented in the clinical notes, including sleep medicine, neurology, or explicitly documented outside sleep-specialist diagnosis; diagnostic evaluation, follow-up, interpretation of sleep testing, treatment planning, or other sleep-disorder management.
(E3) OBJECTIVE TESTING documented in any note: MSLT (MSL, SOREMPs); CSF hypocretin; HLA DQB1*0602; prior PSG with SOREMP. Objective testing performed elsewhere counts if the note explicitly reports its result (e.g. "outside MSLT confirmed narcolepsy"), including when the exact value is masked (see the de-identification guidance below).

=== LABELING PRINCIPLE ===

The study label should summarize the most specific diagnosis documented in the patient's longitudinal clinical record, not re-diagnose the patient from scratch.
When the longitudinal record consistently documents an established sleep disorder diagnosis—whether across multiple encounters or through a clearly documented historical diagnosis—prefer reproducing that diagnosis unless there is clear contradictory evidence, including documented diagnostic revision by the treating specialist, objective testing that leads to a revised diagnosis, or repeated longitudinal documentation supporting an alternative diagnosis.

Do not independently overturn an established specialist diagnosis based on an isolated test result; consider its documented clinical interpretation and any explicit diagnostic revision.

A documented longitudinal diagnosis should generally be preserved. Do not downgrade an established sleep disorder diagnosis to Uncertain solely because the available notes do not contain the original MSLT, CSF, or other diagnostic evidence.
The original diagnostic workup may have occurred outside the available record or before the available notes.

=== DECISION RULES ===

R1. Assign NT1 or NT2 when the longitudinal record clearly documents that subtype. Do NOT require the original diagnostic workup or every ICSD-3 criterion to be reproduced in the available notes. 

R2. Assign IH when the longitudinal record clearly documents an established diagnosis of idiopathic hypersomnia. Do NOT require the original diagnostic workup or objective testing to remain available if the diagnosis is consistently documented. 

A clearly documented established NT2 or IH diagnosis by a sleep specialist may support the corresponding label even in a single available encounter without the original PSG/MSLT report. Tentative diagnoses, differential diagnoses, and plans to evaluate for a disorder do not establish that diagnosis.

R3. Assign Uncertain when the available longitudinal record does not establish narcolepsy or idiopathic hypersomnia and the evidence remains insufficient, ambiguous, conflicting, or under evaluation. This includes hypersomnolence with an incomplete workup or only a suspected alternative explanation. Missing original testing alone does not make an established specialist diagnosis uncertain. 

R4. Assign Not_NT_IH only when the record provides clear evidence against both narcolepsy and idiopathic hypersomnia, including a documented clinical conclusion that another condition explains the hypersomnolence. A coexisting condition such as OSA, or a merely suspected alternative explanation, is insufficient by itself. Interpret testing in its documented clinical context; a test result that does not support narcolepsy alone does not exclude IH. 

R5. Treatment alone is supportive, NOT diagnostic. 

R6. Carry-forward artifact: problem list or copied past medical history mentions narcolepsy, but the note body contains no discussion of narcolepsy, hypersomnolence, cataplexy, diagnostic evaluation, or sleep-specialist management → treat as carry-forward documentation, NOT independent evidence.

R7. De-identification masks some MSLT/diagnostic numbers with asterisks (dates, MSL, SOREMP counts), e.g. "average sleep latency was 0.8 minutes, and she demonstrated 4 *******" ("4 SOREMPs" masked); "confirms the narcolepsy diagnosis with ***<8 minutes and */* ******" (MSL and SOREMP fraction masked). 
A masked value is NOT "absent" and NOT evidence against NT. If the narrative states the test confirms narcolepsy / MSL "<8 minutes" / SOREMPs present, treat it as POSITIVE objective evidence (MSLT_positive=Yes), even though the exact number is unavailable. Do not invent numbers.
